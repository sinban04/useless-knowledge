⚖️ NK Cells

A natural killer cell carries no rearranged receptor and asks no question about a specific peptide. It runs a continuous subtraction — activating signals minus inhibitory signals — and kills only when the balance tips.

Core idea An NK cell decides by integrating opposing germline-encoded receptors, not by recognizing one antigen. Inhibitory receptors read normal self (mostly MHC class I); activating receptors read stress, infection, and antibody coating. Killing happens only when activation outweighs inhibition — so the same NK cell spares a healthy neighbor and destroys a virus-infected or transformed one.
1What an NK cell is

A natural killer (NK) cell is an innate lymphocyte that can kill abnormal host cells without first undergoing antigen-specific clonal expansion. NK cells are especially important early in viral infection and in surveillance against cells that reduce MHC class I expression.

An NK cell does not use a rearranged T-cell receptor or B-cell receptor. Instead it integrates signals from a fixed, germline-encoded set of inhibitory and activating receptors. There is no clonal selection step and no somatic recombination — the receptor repertoire ships from the genome, and the cell's specificity is the net of many receptors firing at once.

NK-cell decision | +--> inhibitory receptors detect normal self signals, often MHC I | +--> activating receptors detect stress ligands, infection signals, antibody Fc | v kill only if activating signals outweigh inhibitory signals

The decision is analog, not a single on/off receptor. The sliders in the animation above make this concrete: neither input alone decides the outcome — only their difference does.

The phosphatase wins ties. Inhibitory ITIMs (immunoreceptor tyrosine-based inhibitory motifs) recruit SHP-1 / SHP-2, which dephosphorylate the very kinases that activating ITAMs are trying to turn on. So inhibition is not a passive absence of activation — it actively erases the activating signal. This is why a healthy cell with plenty of MHC I is safe even if it carries a little stress ligand.
2Missing-self recognition

Many viruses and tumors reduce MHC I expression to evade CD8 T cells. That move creates a vulnerability. NK cells detect the absence of normal inhibitory MHC I signals — the missing-self principle (Kärre and Ljunggren).

healthy nucleated cell normal MHC I -> inhibitory NK receptor (KIR / NKG2A) engaged -> no killing virus-infected or tumor cell low MHC I -> inhibitory signal reduced stress ligands -> activating signals increased result -> NK killing

This complements CD8 T-cell immunity by attacking exactly the escape route T cells cannot follow:

Target strategyCD8 T-cell problemNK-cell advantage
Normal MHC I with viral peptideCD8 T cell can recognizeNK inhibition may dominate unless stress signals are strong
Reduced MHC ICD8 T cell loses peptide-MHC targetNK cell loses inhibitory signal
Stress ligand expressionMay or may not produce a known peptide targetNK activating receptors can respond
The MHC-I trap The system sets a no-win choice for the pathogen: keep MHC I and risk CD8 T-cell detection, or reduce MHC I and risk NK-cell detection. Some viruses (e.g. human cytomegalovirus) try a third path — leaving the non-classical HLA-E up to keep NKG2A inhibited — which is why NK biology and viral immune-evasion co-evolve so visibly.

Drag the MHC I slider down in the animation while leaving stress modest: you can watch inhibition shrink in the balance meter until the verdict flips, even without raising the activating input — that is missing-self in action.

3Induced-self recognition

NK cells also respond to induced-self signals: host molecules upregulated by stress, DNA damage, infection, or transformation. These ligands engage activating NK receptors — chiefly NKG2D, whose ligands MICA/MICB and ULBP1–6 are nearly absent on healthy cells but driven up by the DNA-damage response and oncogenic stress.

The logic differs from PAMP recognition. NK cells are not necessarily detecting a microbial molecule. They are detecting a host cell whose surface declares, "I am stressed, infected, transformed, or damaged."

cell stress (DNA damage, viral infection, transformation) | v stress ligand (MICA/MICB, ULBPs) expression rises | v NK activating receptor (NKG2D) engaged | v kill if inhibition is insufficient

This is useful for cancer surveillance and antiviral defense, but it requires restraint. Too little NK activity lets abnormal cells escape; too much can contribute to inflammatory damage. The animation's verdict logic mirrors this: high stress alone can flip the result to ⚔ KILL only once it overcomes the standing inhibition.

Tumors fight back at the ligand level Many tumors shed soluble MICA/MICB by proteolytic cleavage. Soluble ligand mops up NKG2D and even down-modulates the receptor on NK cells, blunting induced-self recognition — one reason the surveillance arm is not foolproof.

The cytotoxic synapse, step by step

Granule killing is not a spray — it is a directed, polarized event so that only the bound target dies.

STEP 1
Conjugate
Activating receptors win the balance; integrin LFA-1 grips ICAM on the target to form a stable conjugate.
STEP 2
Polarize
The microtubule-organizing center and lytic granules reorient toward the contact — the immunological synapse.
STEP 3
Release
Granules dock and fuse, secreting perforin + granzymes into the narrow synaptic cleft only.
STEP 4
Apoptosis
Granzyme B activates caspases / cleaves Bid; the target dies by apoptosis without spilling its contents.
STEP 5
Detach & recycle
The NK cell detaches, re-arms its granule stores, and can serially kill additional targets.

ADCC: how B cells hand NK cells a target

Antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) connects B cells to NK cells. If antibodies coat a target-cell surface, NK-cell CD16 (FcγRIIIa) binds the antibody Fc region. CD16 signals through ITAM-bearing adaptors (CD3ζ / FcRγ), and — uniquely among NK triggers — engagement of CD16 alone is strong enough to override inhibition and force a kill.

target cell coated with IgG | v NK CD16 (FcγRIIIa) binds Fc portion of IgG | v ITAM signaling -> NK cell releases cytotoxic granules | v target cell dies

This is one reason therapeutic antibodies can recruit immune killing even when they do not directly poison the target — and why antibody engineering tunes the Fc region for CD16 affinity. See B cells & antibodies (chapter 05).

5Cytokines activate NK cells early

NK cells respond to cytokines made by infected tissue, macrophages, and dendritic cells. Important activators include type I interferons (IFN-α/β), IL-12, IL-15, and IL-18 — and each does a distinct job.

NK cells can therefore act during the gap between initial infection and adaptive clonal expansion. Cytokine "priming" raises the cell's baseline so that the same surface-receptor balance now reaches the kill threshold faster — the activation arm starts pre-charged. See the cross-talk with macrophages in chapter 03 and the timing picture in chapter 09.

6NK cells are educated (licensed)

NK cells must avoid killing every normal cell. During development and maturation, NK cells undergo a process often called education or licensing. Cells whose inhibitory receptors can engage self MHC become functionally competent (licensed); cells lacking such interactions are tuned to be hyporesponsive.

The broad idea is calibration, not classroom-style learning. NK receptors are mostly germline-encoded; the NK cell is not rearranging a new receptor the way a T cell or B cell does. Education tunes the response threshold according to the self-MHC signals the NK cell actually experiences.

1
Random receptor mix
An immature NK cell expresses a stochastic combination of inhibitory receptors.
2
Self-MHC test
If one inhibitory receptor binds this body's own MHC I, the cell receives a calibration signal.
3A
Licensed
The cell becomes fully responsive because it has learned what normal inhibition feels like.
3B
Unlicensed
No useful self-MHC match means the cell is tuned down for safety.
4
Missing-self meaning
Only a cell calibrated to depend on self-MHC treats loss of that signal as a strong kill cue.

Licensed (educated)

An inhibitory receptor (e.g. a KIR) found a matching self-MHC I during development. The cell is set fully responsive — and is the one that reacts strongly to missing-self.

Unlicensed (hyporesponsive)

No inhibitory receptor matched a self-MHC ligand. To stay safe the cell is tuned down, so the absence of inhibition it could never sense does not trigger autoreactive killing.

Education is the safety counterpart of missing-self. Missing-self only works if an NK cell was first taught to depend on a specific self-MHC signal. Licensing ensures that the cells most ready to kill on lost inhibition are exactly the cells that were calibrated against real self MHC — preventing them from attacking healthy tissue that simply never expressed a matching ligand. Think of education as a rheostat, not a switch: stronger self-MHC calibration can make the NK cell more responsive when that signal disappears.
7NK cells versus CD8 T cells

NK cells and CD8 T cells can both kill host cells, and they are complementary lymphocyte-lineage killers. A CD8 T cell is an adaptive T lymphocyte with a rearranged TCR. An NK cell is an innate lymphocyte / innate lymphoid cell with germline-encoded receptor sets. They share the cytotoxic granule toolkit, but they decide when to use it by opposite information rules.

The immune system uses both because each covers the other's blind spot: the CD8 T cell needs MHC I to see its target, and the NK cell pounces precisely when MHC I disappears. See T cells (chapter 06) for the CD8 side of this pairing.

Summary NK cells are innate lymphocytes that kill through a balance of inhibitory and activating signals. Inhibitory receptors (KIR, NKG2A/CD94) read MHC I and HLA-E and recruit SHP-1 to erase activation; activating receptors (NKG2D, the NCRs NKp30/44/46, and CD16) read induced-self stress ligands (MICA/MICB, ULBPs) and antibody Fc. They detect missing self when MHC I is reduced, induced self when stress ligands rise, and antibody-coated targets through CD16-mediated ADCC. Education/licensing calibrates them against real self MHC, and cytokine priming (type I IFN, IL-12/15/18) pre-charges the activation arm. NK cells provide early antiviral and antitumor pressure, produce IFN-γ, and complement CD8 T cells by targeting cells that try to escape peptide-MHC recognition.
