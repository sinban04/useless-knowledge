🧩 System Synthesis

The immune system is not one army with stronger and weaker soldiers — it is a layered evidence-processing pipeline. This recap compresses the whole report into a short algorithm, maps it onto the four highlighted cell types, and runs three worked simulations end to end.

Core idea The body answers threats by staging recognition: broad innate sensing first, then peptide evidence, then rare matching clones, then precise effectors, then resolution and memory. Macrophages, B cells, T cells, and NK cells are each incomplete alone — they make sense only as roles inside this escalation pipeline.
🧩The whole system on one rail

Every chapter is one station of this layered evidence-processing pipeline.

1The short algorithm

Strip away the cell names and the immune system collapses into a ten-step program — a recognition-then-action loop that runs every time something breaches a barrier:

1. keep microbes out 2. detect conserved danger or tissue damage 3. tag suspicious targets 4. eat and destroy what can be eaten 5. display peptide evidence to lymphocytes 6. expand rare matching B and T cells 7. coat extracellular targets with antibody 8. kill infected, stressed, or abnormal host cells 9. shut down the response 10. remember useful specificity while preserving tolerance

Each step rests on a distinct molecular machine. Worth naming the hardware explicitly, because the same ten steps reappear in every worked example below:

StepMolecular machinery doing the work
Keep outEpithelial tight junctions, mucus + secretory IgA, lysozyme, gastric acid, antimicrobial peptides (defensins, cathelicidins)
DetectPRRs: TLRs, NLRs (NLRP3 inflammasome), RIG-I/MDA5 for viral RNA, cGAS-STING for cytosolic DNA
TagComplement C3b/iC3b opsonins, antibody Fc, collectins/pentraxins (MBL, C-reactive protein)
Eat & destroyFcγR/CR3-triggered actin engulfment, phagolysosome at pH ~4.5, NADPH-oxidase respiratory burst, iNOS-derived NO
DisplayImmunoproteasome → TAP → MHC I (endogenous); endosomal cathepsins → MHC II via invariant chain/CLIP/HLA-DM (exogenous)
ExpandClonal selection of one BCR/TCR specificity; IL-2 autocrine loop; germinal-center proliferation
CoatClass-switched antibody (IgG, IgA) from plasma cells; neutralization, opsonization, classical-complement triggering
Kill host cellsCD8 perforin/granzyme; Fas-FasL; NK missing-self killing; antibody-dependent ADCC via CD16
Shut downTreg suppression (IL-10, TGF-β, CTLA-4), inhibitory checkpoints (PD-1), effector contraction, efferocytosis
RememberLong-lived memory B/T cells, bone-marrow plasma cells; tolerance preserved by negative selection + Tregs

3Why macrophages matter — the local interpreter

Macrophages are the only highlighted cell already resident in tissue when trouble begins. Their defining act is conversion: turning a local physical event into a tissue-wide inflammatory state.

tissue event -> macrophage interpretation -> inflammatory state | +-- PRRs (TLR/NLR) fire -> NF-κB program on +-- NLRP3 inflammasome -> caspase-1 -> mature IL-1β +-- secretes TNF-α, IL-6, IL-12 + chemokines +-- recruits neutrophils & monocytes along gradients

The failure modes are symmetric and clinically important:

UNDERREACT
Microbes spread
Too little sensing or cytokine output lets infection establish before reinforcements arrive.
OVERREACT
Tissue suffers
Body-wide TNF-α drives the shock and capillary leak of sepsis; collateral damage exceeds the threat.
FAIL TO RESOLVE
Chronic inflammation
Without efferocytosis and a repair switch, the program never terminates — fibrosis, granulomas, plaques.
4Why B cells matter — externalized specificity

B cells solve a logistics problem: a receptor stuck on a membrane can only act where the cell sits. A plasma cell externalizes that same receptor as soluble antibody, so one specificity can travel through every fluid compartment.

one BCR specificity | v clonal expansion + germinal-center affinity maturation | (somatic hypermutation, class switch) v secreted antibody with the same binding specificity | v neutralization · opsonization · complement · ADCC · mucosal IgA

This is the molecular reason vaccines work. Antibody present before exposure can neutralize a pathogen at the barrier — blocking step 1 of the algorithm before the slower clonal-expansion steps ever need to run. The five effector functions are not interchangeable: neutralization blocks entry, opsonization and complement mark for phagocytes, ADCC arms NK cells, and secretory IgA guards mucosal surfaces. See B cells & antibodies.

5Why T cells matter — the context-dependent decision layer

T cells cannot see inside a cell directly. The trick is indirect: MHC molecules continuously sample the cell's protein contents and display peptide fragments on the surface, and T cells read that display.

cell interior -> peptide processing -> MHC display -> T-cell decision | | +-- MHC I (proteasome/TAP, all cells) --> CD8 kills infected cell +-- MHC II (endosome, APCs only) --> CD4 chooses the program

The two T-cell lineages answer two different problems:

CD8 — the executioner

Solves the intracellular pathogen problem. A cell making viral protein loads viral peptide on MHC I; the CD8 cell delivers perforin/granzyme and Fas-FasL signals to kill the factory before it finishes producing virus.

CD4 — the conductor

Solves the coordination problem. By choosing a helper program — TH1 (macrophage activation), TFH (antibody maturation), TH17 (neutrophil/barrier), or Treg (suppression) — CD4 cells decide which weapon the whole system expands.

This is why T cells are the most powerful context-dependent layer: the same peptide can drive killing, antibody help, or tolerance depending on which T cell reads it and what costimulation accompanies it. See T cells and antigen presentation.

6Why NK cells matter — covering the surveillance blind spot

The MHC-I display that CD8 cells depend on has an exploit: a virus or tumor that downregulates MHC I goes invisible to CD8 surveillance. NK cells close this gap by inverting the logic — they kill cells that lack the normal "self" signal.

normal self display present (MHC I high) -> inhibitory receptor engaged -> NK inhibition (spare the cell) self display missing OR stress ligands high (MIC-A/B) -> activating signal wins the balance -> NK activation (kill)

NK cells are not a primitive proto-T-cell; they answer a different question — "does this cell still look like healthy self?" rather than "what peptide is this cell showing?" Two properties make them complementary: they act early, before antigen-specific T-cell expansion peaks, and they can kill antibody-coated targets through CD16 (ADCC), bridging back to the B-cell response. See NK cells.

7Three worked mental simulations

The real test of the model is running it forward on concrete threats. The same ten-step algorithm produces three very different choreographies depending on where the pathogen lives.

Extracellular bacterium

macrophage/complement detect -> neutrophils and inflammation contain -> B cells produce antibody -> antibody opsonizes (C3b + Fc tags) -> macrophages & neutrophils clear more efficiently -> memory B cells and plasma cells remain

Most important highlighted cell: the B cell, with macrophages as early sentinels and antibody-guided effectors. The pathogen lives in fluids, so the fluid-borne weapon (antibody) dominates.

Virus inside epithelial cells

infected cells produce interferon -> NK cells kill stressed or MHC-low cells early -> dendritic cells prime CD8 and CD4 T cells -> CD8 T cells kill peptide-MHC I positive infected cells -> B cells make neutralizing antibodies against free virus -> memory B and T cells remain

Most important highlighted cells: T cells and NK cells, with B cells blocking spread outside cells. Because the threat hides inside host cells, the host-cell killers lead while antibody mops up virus in transit.

Intracellular bacterium in macrophages

macrophage eats bacterium but cannot fully kill -> macrophage presents peptide on MHC II -> TH1 cell recognizes and releases IFN-γ -> macrophage becomes more microbicidal (ROS, NO) -> chronic persistence can form granulomas

Most important highlighted cells: macrophages and CD4 TH1 cells. The eater cannot finish the kill alone — it needs a licensing signal from the decision layer, the clearest example of cell-to-cell collaboration in the whole report. See the TH1 feedback loop.

8The final model — a layered evidence-processing system

Stepping all the way back, the report's central claim is architectural. Each layer asks one question; the answer decides whether the threat escalates to the next layer. The cells are simply the agents that staff these layers.

LayerMain questionRepresentative mechanisms
BarrierCan entry be prevented?Skin, mucus, acid, cilia, microbiota
Innate sensorDoes this look like damage or conserved microbial pattern?PRRs, complement, macrophages, dendritic cells
Innate effectorCan the threat be contained immediately?Phagocytosis, inflammation, neutrophils, NK cells
Antigen presentationWhat molecular evidence should lymphocytes inspect?MHC I, MHC II, dendritic-cell migration
Adaptive selectionWhich rare clone matches?BCR/TCR clonal selection
Adaptive effectorWhat precise weapon should be expanded?Antibody, CD8 killing, CD4 help
RegulationHow do we avoid host damage?Tregs, checkpoints, contraction, resolution
MemoryHow do we improve next time?Memory B/T cells, plasma cells
BARRIER ── INNATE SENSOR ── INNATE EFFECTOR ── ANTIGEN PRESENTATION | v MEMORY ◄── REGULATION ◄── ADAPTIVE EFFECTOR ◄── ADAPTIVE SELECTION

Macrophages, B cells, T cells, and NK cells make sense only inside this architecture. Each is incomplete by itself; together they let the body respond quickly, specialize accurately, kill selectively, resolve damage, and remember. The overview animation at the top of this page traces exactly this rail — a threat token flowing from barrier to memory.

Summary The immune system works by staging recognition. Macrophages and innate sensors detect broad danger and shape the tissue response. Antigen-presenting cells convert local events into peptide evidence. B cells turn receptor specificity into antibodies. T cells inspect peptide-MHC displays and coordinate or execute cellular responses. NK cells kill stressed or MHC-low cells before or alongside adaptive immunity. Memory improves the next response, and tolerance keeps the system from becoming self-destructive.
