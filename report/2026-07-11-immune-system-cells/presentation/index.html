Speed versus specificity

Two clocks explain most immune-response confusion

Innate clock: minutes to hours

  • Receptors are germline-encoded and pre-built.
  • PRRs, complement, macrophages, neutrophils, and NK cells act before clonal expansion.
  • Output: containment, inflammation, interferon, early killing.

Adaptive clock: days on first exposure

  • BCR and TCR specificities are generated by V(D)J recombination.
  • The rare matching clone must be found, licensed, and expanded.
  • Output: antibody, CD4 help, CD8 killing, durable memory.
Fast sensors tell the slow clonal system which match is worth expanding.
Innate is fixed and fastAdaptive is selected and precise
Routing rule

Location decides the weapon

Extracellular

Free microbes or toxins

Antibody and complement neutralize, coat, and hand targets to phagocytes.

Vesicular

Microbes inside phagosomes

MHC II shows eaten material to CD4 TH1 cells, which activate macrophages.

Cytosolic

Virus inside host machinery

MHC I displays intracellular peptides to CD8 T cells for focused killing.

MHC-low

Escape from CD8 inspection

NK cells detect missing self and stress ligands without clonal expansion.

Sterile damage

Broken tissue, no microbe

DAMPs trigger macrophages, inflammasome signals, recruitment, and repair.

Antibody cannot inspect the cytosol. CD8 cannot see free toxin. NK cells do not usually kill loose antigen. Compartment matters.

Permission layer

Innate sensing decides whether specificity is safe to expand

Pattern-recognition receptors

TLRs, NLRs, RIG-I/MDA5, and cGAS-STING read conserved microbial or misplaced-self signals.

Complement

Soluble plasma proteins test surfaces and deposit sticky opsonin on targets that lack host protection.

Interferon

Viral nucleic-acid sensors induce type I interferon, warning nearby cells and priming NK and CD8 responses.

Early cleanup crew

Macrophages interpret tissue context; neutrophils arrive as short-lived attackers

Macrophage

  • Resident sentinel in tissue.
  • Reads PRRs, Fc tags, complement tags, and damage signals.
  • Eats, releases cytokines, recruits help, resolves debris.

Neutrophil

  • Bone-marrow-derived myeloid granulocyte.
  • Follows CXCL8, C5a, and bacterial gradients from blood into tissue.
  • Phagocytosis, respiratory burst, granules, and NETs.

The peptide is not handed to CD3. TCR binds outside; CD3 and coreceptor machinery convert that binding event into phosphorylation inside.

Why weak binding helps

Fast scanning, serial engagement, and kinetic proofreading. Bonds must last long enough to finish the phosphorylation timer.

Why context still matters

A strong pMHC match is only signal 1 for a naive T cell. Costimulation and cytokines decide expansion and fate.

Prepared before exposure

B and T receptors are prepared statistically, not custom-built after infection

Before antigen
RAG1/2 cuts and rejoins V, D, and J gene segments during B- and T-cell development.
Diversity
Combinatorial choice, junctional diversity, TdT additions, and chain pairing build a huge repertoire.

Antigen selects an existing clone; it does not design the first receptor from scratch.

If no clone binds well enough, the first response may be weak. Diversity is statistical coverage, not a guarantee.

Complementary lymphocyte killers

NK and CD8 T cells close each other's blind spots

CD8 T cell

  • Adaptive T lymphocyte with rearranged TCR.
  • Reads specific peptide on MHC I.
  • Powerful after clonal expansion and memory formation.
  • Problem: loses the target when MHC I is downregulated.

NK cell

  • Innate lymphocyte with germline-encoded receptor sets.
  • Reads missing self, stress ligands, and antibody Fc.
  • Fast early response without antigen-specific expansion.
  • Advantage: punishes the MHC-loss escape route.
MHC I present
CD8 can inspect peptide-MHC I. If the peptide matches, CTL killing is precise.
MHC I missing
CD8 loses the display, but NK inhibitory signaling drops. Stress plus missing self can trigger NK killing.
Keep MHC I: CD8 sees peptideLose MHC I: NK sees missing self
Long-term response

Memory is stored in several formats

B-cell memory

Memory B cell

Resting clone that can re-enter response and germinal-center-like improvement after re-exposure.

Antibody floor

Long-lived plasma cell

Bone-marrow resident factory that secretes antibody without re-seeing antigen.

T-cell memory

Central / effector memory

Expanded, primed T-cell pools in lymph nodes, blood, and tissues.

Barrier memory

Tissue-resident T cell

Memory T cells parked in lung, gut, skin, and other entry sites for rapid local response.

B-cell memory can leave antibody already floating. T-cell memory leaves more antigen-specific cells, already primed for faster action.
Memory B and T cells differPlasma cells are active factories
Safety system

Tolerance is the price of powerful recognition

Central tolerance

Bone marrow and thymus delete or divert strongly self-reactive clones before release.

Peripheral tolerance

Tregs, anergy, deletion, and missing costimulation restrain escaped clones in tissue.

Checkpoints

CTLA-4 and PD-1 reduce activation when immune damage would exceed benefit.

Failure mode

Autoimmunity, allergy, immunodeficiency, cancer evasion, or chronic inflammation.

Clinical tradeoff

Checkpoint blockade can restore antitumor T cells but also releases self-reactive damage.

Resolution

Contraction, efferocytosis, and repair programs stop the response after the threat is contained.

Diversity creates riskBrakes make diversity usable
Run the model

Three encounters use the same routing rules

Bacterial splinter

Extracellular plus damaged tissue
  1. Macrophages and complement detect.
  2. Neutrophils arrive fast.
  3. B cells make antibody.
  4. Antibody and C3b opsonize for phagocytes.

Respiratory virus

Cytosolic infection plus free virus
  1. Viral RNA/DNA triggers interferon.
  2. NK cells kill stressed or MHC-low cells early.
  3. CD8 T cells kill peptide-MHC-I-positive cells.
  4. Antibody neutralizes free virus.

Vaccination

Antigen plus safe innate context
  1. Adjuvant activates antigen-presenting cells.
  2. B and T clones expand without full disease.
  3. Germinal centers improve antibody.
  4. Memory and plasma cells remain.
Ask where the threat livesThen predict the dominant arm
Final model

Keep the algorithm, not the vocabulary

Macrophages sense and clean locally. Dendritic cells carry evidence. B cells externalize specificity as antibody. T cells inspect peptide-MHC and coordinate or kill. NK cells punish stressed or MHC-low cells. Memory improves the next response, and tolerance keeps the system from turning inward.
